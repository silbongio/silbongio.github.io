I was born and raised in Buenos Aires, Argentina. I completed my undergraduate degree at the <a href="http://www.filo.uba.ar">Universidad de Buenos Aires (Facultad de Filosofía y Letras)</a>, where I studied <i>Letras</i>. I showed up my very first day ready to study literature, but had one class in Linguistics and the rest is history. 

I earned a dual-PhD in <a href="http://www.indiana.edu/~lingdept/" target="_blank">Linguistics </a> and <a href="http://www.indiana.edu/~spanport/" target="_blank">Hispanic Linguistics</a> at Indiana University, under the supervision of Prof. <a href="http://cl.indiana.edu/~kdejong/" target="_blank">Kenneth de Jong </a> and Prof. <a href="https://indiana.academia.edu/ErikWillis" target="_blank">Erik W. Willis</a>. My main area of research is experimental phonology: I study the phonetic underpinnings of phonological variation, with a focus on Spanish. I have also published work in sociolinguistics and second language acquisition.

I am currently Associate Professor of Spanish, in the <a href="https://rcs.msu.edu/" target="_blank">Department of Romance and Classical Studies</a> at Michigan State University. I am also adjunct faculty in the Linguistics program, core faculty in the <a href= "https://clacs.isp.msu.edu/" target="_blank">Center of Latin American Studies</a> and affiliated in <a href="https://sls.msu.edu/" target="_blank">Second Language Studies</a> and <a href="https://digitalhumanities.msu.edu/" target="_blank">Digital Humanities</a>. So many links! But now you know: cool people all around me!
